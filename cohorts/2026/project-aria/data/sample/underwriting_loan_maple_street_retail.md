# Loan Underwriting File — Maple Street Retail Partners LLC

**Loan type:** C&I term loan, leasehold improvements
**Loan amount:** $640,000
**Origination date:** 2026-04-09
**Underwriter:** T. Osei, Commercial Lender

## Repayment Source Analysis

Primary repayment source is lease income from three retail tenants under
long-term leases (weighted average remaining term 6.2 years), assigned to
the bank as additional security. Rent roll reviewed and reconciled to
executed lease agreements on file. Guarantor personal financial statement
and two years of personal tax returns reviewed; guarantor global cash flow
supports the credit independently of property performance. Debt service
coverage ratio of 1.41x calculated on in-place rent roll, above the 1.20x
C&I policy minimum.

## Cash Flow Analysis

Borrower is a single-purpose leasing entity with no independent operations
beyond collecting and remitting rent; a full operating cash flow statement
is not applicable in the traditional sense. In lieu of one, the file
contains a rent-roll-based debt service schedule (see Repayment Source
Analysis above) projecting coverage through loan maturity under three
vacancy scenarios (0%, 15%, 30%). No separate stress-tested cash flow
statement or downside liquidity analysis was prepared for the borrowing
entity itself.

## Collateral Valuation

Collateral is a first lien on the retail property at 214-218 Maple Street,
plus an assignment of leases and rents. Independent MAI appraisal on file
(dated 2026-02-18, appraised value $1,020,000, income approach weighted
primary). Loan-to-value of 62.7%, within the bank's policy limit for
income-producing C&I real estate. Environmental Phase I on file, no
recognized environmental conditions noted. Title and lien searches current
and clean.

## Underwriter's recommendation

Approve. Note for file: because the borrower is a single-purpose leasing
entity, standard operating-company cash flow analysis does not apply here —
confirm at next renewal whether a lease-rollover stress scenario (rather
than a traditional cash flow statement) should be the documented standard
for this loan type going forward, so future examiners don't read the
absence of a conventional cash flow statement as a gap without that
context on file.
