# Regulatory Memorandum of Understanding — Northfield Mutual Bank

**Document type:** Internal compliance summary
**Prepared by:** Compliance Program Office
**Date:** 2026-06-01
**Status:** Active — remediation in progress

## Background

Following its most recent safety-and-soundness examination, Northfield
Mutual received a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) from its state banking
regulator, with FDIC concurrence. The MOU is a binding enforcement action
requiring the Board to remediate specific deficiencies on fixed timelines,
followed by ongoing quarterly progress reporting until regulators agree to
terminate it. This is a routine, non-public supervisory instrument common in
community banking — it is not a lawsuit, and no monetary penalty attaches to
it directly, but failure to remediate on schedule can escalate to a formal
Consent Order.

## Findings requiring remediation

1. **Credit quality deterioration** — classified/problem loans grew without
   a structured reduction plan or clear ownership.
2. **Loan grading inconsistency** — risk ratings were not being reassessed
   regularly or consistently against standard criteria; deteriorating
   credits were not reclassified in time.
3. **Weak underwriting documentation** — insufficient analysis of borrower
   repayment sources, cash flow, and collateral valuation at the time of
   loan origination or renewal, across a sample of files reviewed by
   examiners. *(This finding is the subject of the underwriting exception
   tracking below.)*
4. **No formal concentration limits** — no board-approved caps on industry
   concentration (agriculture, C&I) or single-borrower exposure relative to
   capital.
5. **Capital adequacy risk** — leverage capital ratio trending toward the
   regulatory floor with no documented plan to maintain a buffer.
6. **Liquidity risk** — insufficient forward-looking cash flow and stress
   testing to catch liquidity pressure before it becomes acute.
7. **Interest rate risk gaps** — inconsistent policy limits between risk
   management frameworks.
8. **Governance and reporting weaknesses** — errors in regulatory call
   reporting, incomplete board meeting documentation, and no systematic
   tracking of open compliance obligations. *(This finding is the subject of
   the board/regulatory reporting remediation below.)*

## Remediation structure

Each finding above carries either a 30-day or 60-day initial remediation
deadline, followed by ongoing quarterly progress reporting to the Board and
the regulator until the finding is closed and the MOU is terminated. Status
of individual remediation items is tracked in
[`mou_remediation_tracker.md`](mou_remediation_tracker.md).

## Scope note

This document set covers two of the eight findings in depth — underwriting
documentation (finding 3) and board/regulatory reporting (finding 8) —
because they are the findings this phase of the compliance program is
actively remediating. The other six findings exist in the corpus only at
the summary level above; loan-grading, concentration, capital, and
liquidity monitoring are a separate, not-yet-built phase of the program.
